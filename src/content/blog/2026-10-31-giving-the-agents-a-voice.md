Text is the right default for most of what the agents send me. But not all of it.

Some things are better heard than read. A morning digest while I am making coffee. A long answer when I am walking and not looking at the screen. A quick confirmation I do not want to stop and read. For those, the agents can speak. They render the reply to audio and send it as a voice note, with a short text summary underneath so I can still skim if I want.

The setup is plain. There is a text-to-speech step the agents can call, a default voice I settled on after trying several, and a rule about when to use it instead of text. Voice is opt-in per message, not the default, because a wall of synthesized speech for something I could have read in five seconds is worse than text, not better.

What I like about it is small and specific. The agents feel less like a command line and more like something you can have in your ear while your hands are busy.

What I am still unsure about is how much I actually reach for it. Some weeks a lot, some weeks not at all. It is the one capability I am least sure earns its keep, and I am leaving it in partly to find out. That is an honest place to be with a feature, and I would rather say so than pretend every piece of this was an obvious win.

So far this series has been me talking to the agents, one way or another. The next post is about the opposite: the night they started doing work without me asking.
